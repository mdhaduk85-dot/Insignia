3 Guides

Eye Contact is Essential

In ASL, maintaining eye contact with your conversation partner is not just polite — it's grammatically important. Facial expressions and eye gaze carry critical grammatical information like questions, negations, and topic markers. Breaking eye contact is equivalent to turning away mid-sentence.

Use Your Signing Space

The "signing space" is the area roughly from your waist to the top of your head and shoulder-width on each side. Consistent use of this space makes your signs readable. Objects and people can be "placed" in this space and referred back to — a spatial grammar unique to signed languages.

Repetition Builds Fluency

Like any physical skill, muscle memory is key. Practice fingerspelling 5–10 minutes daily, even when not in a full conversation. Use a mirror to check your handshapes. Recording yourself and comparing to reference videos is one of the fastest paths to improving accuracy.
